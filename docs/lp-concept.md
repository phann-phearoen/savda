The key question the page should answer is not “What can I buy?” but:
“What is Savda, and why should I follow it?”

Recommended landing-page structure
1. Hero — Discovery
Lead with the idea of discovering what Cambodia makes.
Visually, this should immediately show beautiful, unexpected Cambodian products rather than a shopping interface.
Core message:
Discover what Cambodia makes.

Then a short explanation of Savda as a platform that tells the stories behind Cambodian-made products.
Primary CTA: Explore Savda
2. The Discovery Feed
Immediately demonstrate that Savda is alive.
A visual grid/feed of products, creators, workshops, food, design, fashion, etc.
The important thing is that these should look like editorial content, not product listings.
Think:
“We found this Cambodian maker…”

rather than:
“Product — $24.99 — Add to cart.”

This is probably the most important section after the hero.
3. The Stories Behind the Products
Introduce the philosophy:
Good products deserve to be discovered.
Good creators deserve to be known.

Show that Savda isn't merely cataloguing products. It tells the stories of the people who make them.
This is where you establish the human side of Savda.
4. Made in Cambodia, Worth Knowing
A broader brand statement.
This section should communicate the underlying discovery problem without sounding like a political or patriotic campaign:
There are Cambodian products you haven't heard of yet.

Then show a diverse selection to prove the point.
This is where Savda starts building the association:
Cambodian-made ≠ cheap alternative.
It can mean design, craftsmanship, innovation, quality, culture, and originality.
5. Discover → Shop
Only after establishing the media/discovery proposition should you introduce commerce.
Something like:
Found something you like?
Shop products featured by Savda.

Then a relatively restrained preview of Savda Shop.
This makes shopping feel like the natural next step rather than the reason the platform exists.
6. For Creators
A section aimed at makers:
You make it. We tell the story.

Explain at a high level that Savda helps Cambodian creators get discovered and gives interested audiences a way to purchase their products.
This is important because creators are effectively Savda's content supply and commerce supply simultaneously.
7. Follow Savda
End with the thing that is actually most valuable to the business:
the audience.
Something like:
Keep discovering.
Follow Savda for Cambodian products, creators, and stories worth knowing.

Social links / follow CTA.
The overall flow
I'd structure the narrative as:
LOOK
↓
DISCOVER
↓
MEET THE CREATOR
↓
APPRECIATE
↓
SHOP  
Not:
SEARCH → FILTER → PRODUCT → CART
That distinction should be visible in the website design itself.
Visual direction
Given the Savda / សាវតា identity we've been exploring, I'd make the landing page:
- Editorial
- Image-heavy
- Minimal
- Warm and contemporary
- Strong Khmer identity without traditional Cambodian decoration
- More magazine / Instagram / documentary than marketplace
- Product photography rather than UI-heavy product cards
And I would give the negative-space/continuous-line ស mark plenty of room to breathe.
